## PART ONE: DESIGN DIRECTION HIERARCHY

The fitted. feed should feel like the social presence of a thoughtful editorial brand that genuinely cares about the people it's talking to. Not a product that posts. A voice that thinks out loud.

Three references, in priority order:

---

### 1. Eszter Bial — Lead Aesthetic (Warmth, Air, Personhood)
*This is the dominant register. Every post should feel primarily Bial.*

**What it brings:** Airy elegance. Generous whitespace. Personal warmth that doesn't tip into sentimentality. Thoughtful interactivity — content that feels like it's in conversation with you, not performing at you. Bial's work reads as if someone left a beautiful thought on a well-lit table.

**How it shows up in fitted.:**
- Covers with a single dominant idea and intentional negative space — not a list, not a crowded slide
- Whitespace is structural — it creates emphasis and rest. If a design feels full, remove something.
- The "fitted. thinks:" prompt functions like a handwritten note left on a table: intimate, not corporate. **Never appears as a label on the actual Instagram grid post** — it's an internal scripting guide and Reel scene direction only.
- Stories feel like a quiet check-in from someone who respects your attention
- Reels have long enough scene holds to breathe — 5–7 seconds on the hook scene is right
- Content invites the viewer into an idea rather than presenting at them
- Every post should feel like a thoughtful contribution worth saving or sharing — never like an ad

**The test:** Does this feel like it was made by a human with a point of view? Or does it feel like content?

**Strengthening Bial in practice:**
- Use more Cormorant Garamond Italic in covers — the italic carries warmth that DM Serif Display alone doesn't
- Add a short, personal line to BtS and TS captions — something that sounds like a person, not a brand
- Let questions land without answering them immediately — the viewer's pause is part of the experience
- Interactivity should feel generous, not tactical: "I genuinely want to know" over "drop your thoughts below"

---

### 2. Monocle — Editorial Intelligence (Structure, Confidence, Global Calm)

**What it brings:** Sophisticated editorial intelligence. Calm confidence in the information being shared. A sense that every word was chosen — nothing is filler. Typography as part of the argument, not decoration.

**How it shows up in fitted.:**
- Data and analysis presented without hedging or softening — just clarity
- Strong editorial labels (DATA POINT / QUIET TRUTH, INTENTIONAL MOVES) used the way Monocle uses section titles: establishing voice, not just category
- Carousel flow that reads like a well-edited article — one idea per spread, no repetition
- Captions that make one point, then stop

**The test:** Does this read like something worth keeping? Or does it read like a post that disappears?

---

### 3. Studio Yoke — Punctuation Only (Use Minimally)
*Not a default register. A finishing tool.*

**What it brings:** Visual restraint that creates emphasis through absence. Useful in very specific moments where the number or the word is the entire message.

**Reduced scope (v2.1):** Yoke is now reserved for exactly two use cases:
- **EP pricing slides:** One number. Thin rule. Wordmark. Nothing else.
- **AC score reveals:** The number large, centered, nothing competing.

Yoke should never be the register for an entire post. If a design feels cold, remote, or lifeless, Yoke has crept too far into the piece. Pull it back to Bial immediately.

**The test:** If you removed this restraint and added one warm element — does it get better? If yes, add the warm element.

---

### The Hierarchy in Practice

When making a design decision, apply in order:

1. **Does this feel warm, personal, and generous?** (Bial) — If no, add more air, soften a label, let the whitespace breathe. This question comes first, every time.
2. **Is the intelligence clear?** (Monocle) — If no, tighten the language, sharpen the argument, make it worth keeping.
3. **Is the layout doing too much?** (Yoke, lightly) — Remove the weakest element. But stop before it gets cold.

**Non-negotiable:** Nothing should feel like an ad. If a post could be mistaken for sponsored content from a career tool, revise it until it feels like a thoughtful editorial contribution instead. The bar is: would someone save this even if they weren't looking for a job right now?

The feed is warm and alive. Dark, cold, or repetitive is the failure mode. Quiet is not the same as withdrawn.

## PART TWO: CONTENT TYPE REFINEMENTS

Each type respects its Vibe Template (color, typography, pattern). These notes layer the new aesthetic priority on top.

---

### SI — Something Inspiring / Thoughtful
**Bg:** Ink `#1a1a1f` | **Highlight:** Insight

**Design register:** Bial + Monocle. The SI post should feel like a well-lit thought at 11pm — intimate, considered, worth sitting with. Off-white text on ink. Generous breathing room. The "fitted. thinks:" label should feel like the beginning of a quiet monologue, not a category badge.

**Tone refinement:** More declarative, less coach-y. Not "here's what every job seeker learns" — more "the search changes when you know this one thing." The idea earns its weight on its own terms.

**Format note:** Reel. 3–4 scenes only. First scene holds longest. Each scene one thought, not two.

---

### SE — Something Educational / Reframe
**Bg:** Taupe `#b8a99a` | **Highlight:** Clarity

**Design register:** Monocle backbone, Bial delivery. The SE carousel should read like a short, sharply edited article. One strong idea per slide. No recap slide — each slide advances. The "SOMETHING EDUCATIONAL" label should feel like a section header in a smart magazine, not a content-type tag.

**Tone refinement:** Reframe first, explain second. The setup is almost always a thing people believe that isn't quite right. The carousel unpacks it without performing surprise. Calm, factual, specific.

**Format note:** Carousel, 5–6 slides. Slide 1 = the reframe stated plainly. Slide 2 = why the common assumption is incomplete. Slides 3–4 = evidence. Slide 5 = what this changes. Close slide = one clean line, no summary.

---

### TS — Tell a Story / Customer Story
**Bg:** Navy `#2f3e5c` | **Highlight:** Reflections

**Design register:** Bial entirely. TS is the warmest content type — it's a person's experience, handled with care. Off-white Cormorant Garamond Italic on navy for the opening scene. The story should feel held, not performed.

**Tone refinement:** No composite character should feel like an archetype. They should feel like a specific person with a specific detail — "applied to 22 roles," "12 years in finance," "three years out." The specificity is what makes it feel real. No motivational resolution — the ending is accurate, not inspiring.

**Openers (added v2.1):** "Have you ever…" openers are welcome and often the right choice. They shift the post from a story about someone else to a shared experience. Use when the experience is broadly recognizable — "Have you ever submitted an application feeling good about it, then heard nothing?" This brings the viewer into the story before the character appears.

**fitted. integration:** Weave fitted. into the story naturally, at the point where it actually entered — not before, not as a preamble. The tool should appear as a turning point in the narrative, not as the premise. "She ran the role through fitted. three days after submitting" is natural. "She used fitted., an AI career platform, to…" is not.

**Format note:** Reel, 4 scenes. Scene 1 = hook — either a specific detail or a "Have you ever…" opener. Scene 4 = honest, grounded close — not triumphant, just true. fitted. mentioned at the natural story turning point, not before.

---

### BtS — Behind the Scenes
**Bg:** Off-white `#f4f2ed` | **Highlight:** Craft

**Design register:** Bial almost completely. BtS is the founder's voice — it should feel like a thoughtful newsletter entry, not a startup update. Navy text on off-white. No label in Scene 1 — the personal register is the label.

**Tone refinement:** The thing that happened, without dramatizing it. Not "I almost got it wrong" as a hook that resolves heroically — but "here's the actual decision and what it surfaced." Building in public means the unfinished version is the honest version.

**Format note:** Reel, 4 scenes. First person throughout. Scene 1: no label, just the thought. Scene 4: quiet, forward-facing, not resolved.

---

### EP — Elegant Promotion
**Bg:** Blue `#5171bf` | **Highlight:** Presence

**Design register:** Bial warmth over Monocle structure. Yoke used only on the pricing close slide. EP posts should feel like a generous explanation from someone who wants you to understand what you're getting — not a sales pitch. If it reads as promotional, it needs another revision pass.

**Scope (expanded in v2.1):** EP is not just pricing and plans. It includes:
- **Pricing and plan explanations** (Founding Members, Pro, resume slot) — stated matter-of-factly as product decisions, not offers
- **Feature spotlights** — a single feature explained with depth: what it does, how it works, who it's most useful for, with a real or plausible example
- **Tips and tricks** — how to get the most out of fitted., things people might not discover on their own (e.g., running the same resume against two versions of the same job description to test which framing scores higher)
- **"What Pro/Premium unlocks"** — explained through use cases, not a feature list

**EP is never:** a discount countdown, a limited-time urgency push, or a feature list without context.

**Format note:** Carousel (most common), 5 slides. Reel works well for feature spotlights and tips — show the thing in action. Slide 1 / Scene 1 = the useful insight or capability named plainly. The rest = the logic and the context. Close = pricing (Yoke: number, rule, wordmark only) or a single clean invitation line.

---

### AQ — Ask a Question
**Bg:** Pale Taupe `#d9cfc7` | **Highlight:** Space

**Design register:** Bial entirely. AQ is the most conversational content type — it should feel like a question a thoughtful person actually wants to ask, not a content strategy prompt. Pale taupe with navy text feels like an open notebook page, a question written and left out.

**Tone refinement:** One specific question, not a category ("what's hard about job searching"). The question should surface a tension the audience already feels but hasn't named. Drop it, then leave room. The comments are the content.

**Feature prompt (added v2.1):** Include in AQ Reels, toward the end of Scene 4: *"Stay until the end — we feature real answers on our channel."* This is a genuine invitation, not a manipulative hook. It should feel like the beginning of a community, not a gimmick.

**Demo resumes for early posts:** When no real user data is available yet, build "fitted. thinks:" examples around plausible demo resumes. The example should feel specific enough to be real — a named function, a real-sounding role, a specific gap. Never generic ("marketing professional, 5 years experience"). Always specific ("senior product marketer applying to a director of growth role at a B2B SaaS company").

**Format note:** Reel, 3–4 scenes. Scene 1 = the question, held. Scene 2 = why this specific question, not a generic version. Scene 3 = the texture of the problem (without answering it). Scene 4 = genuine invitation + feature prompt.

---

### DP — Data Point / Quiet Truth
**Bg:** Navy `#2f3e5c` | **Highlight:** Insight

**Design register:** Monocle + Bial. The data should be presented with the confidence Monocle uses for hard numbers — no softening, no "interestingly." But the framing should feel warm and human, not clinical. The number is a lens, not a verdict.

**Flexible formats (see Part Three for full concepts):**
- Data carousel (standard) — one number per slide, building an argument
- Quote carousel — surprising truth stated as a pull quote, no graph required
- Word/definition reel — redefining a career term through a data lens
- Single-image post — one number, maximum whitespace (Yoke moment)

---

### AC — AI Career Coach Spotlight
**Bg:** Off-white `#f4f2ed` | **Highlight:** Clarity

**Design register:** Monocle intelligence, Bial warmth. The AC post shows fitted.'s AI reading — which means it needs to feel accurate and specific, not promotional. The "AI CAREER COACH SPOTLIGHT" label should feel like a section header in a report, not a product feature callout.

**Tone refinement:** Never "fitted. can help you." Always: "here's the specific thing it saw, and here's what changed." The honesty of the read is the point. Post-launch, the examples should come from real or plausible user sessions, not generic demonstrations.

**Format note:** Reel, 4 scenes. Concrete case — person, role, score, specific gap identified, specific change, result. No preamble. Scene 1 = the surprising result or finding.

---

### IM — Intentional Moves
**Bg:** Taupe `#b8a99a` | **Highlight:** Space

**Design register:** Bial entirely. IM is the most personal, philosophical content type. Taupe background with navy and off-white feels warm and grounded — like good advice from someone who doesn't need anything from you. No urgency. No resolution. Just a considered thought about how a search is conducted.

**Tone refinement:** Not tips. Not steps. Observations. "The difference shows up three months later." "The move most people make two months too late." These are quiet, specific truths about behavior — not prescriptions.

**Flexible formats (see Part Four for full concepts):**
- Reel with internal monologue structure (standard)
- Quote carousel — spare, near-poetic observations about intentionality
- Word/definition reel — redefining "strategic," "ready," "qualified"
- Question sequence reel — three quiet questions that do the work

## PART FIVE: SMART BREVITY APPLICATION GUIDE

Smart Brevity is the Axios framework for information that moves fast without sacrificing depth. The core structure:

> **Hook** → **Lede** → **Why It Matters** → **(optional) The Details** → **Done.**

The fitted. content system already applies most of this instinctively. What Smart Brevity adds: a discipline for *where the content earns the next sentence* — and where it should stop instead.

---

### For Reels (target: 15–35 seconds)

The biggest single improvement Smart Brevity makes to Reels: each scene earns the next one or the Reel is too long.

**Scene architecture:**

| Scene | Smart Brevity role | Length target |
|-------|-------------------|---------------|
| 1 | Hook — one complete, surprising thought | 5–7 seconds |
| 2 | Lede — what this is about and why now | 7–9 seconds |
| 3 | Why it matters — the insight that makes this worth watching | 7–9 seconds |
| 4 | (Optional) Supporting evidence or contrast | 5–7 seconds |
| 5 | CTA — minimal; does not restate the lede | 3–4 seconds |

**If a scene can be removed without losing the argument: remove it.** The 4-scene Reel is almost always better than the 5-scene version.

**Hook rules (applied to fitted.):**
- One complete thought, not a question that teases an answer
- Specific over general — "She applied to 22 roles. Heard nothing." not "Have you ever sent a resume and wondered what happened?"
- No "In this video, I'll explain..." — the first scene IS the explanation beginning

**Lede rules:**
- Answers: what is this about, and why does it matter right now
- One or two sentences — not a scene-length setup
- No preamble — starts mid-thought

**Why It Matters — the scene most Reels skip:**
- This is where the insight lives — not the data, but what the data means
- "The search gets shorter — not because she got lucky, but because she got specific." ← this is a Why It Matters line
- Should feel like the thing the viewer remembers

**What to cut:**
- Any scene that restates something said in the hook
- Context scenes that don't advance the argument
- Scene 5 CTAs longer than two sentences

**15–25 second Reel structure (for AQ, IM, and some SI posts):**

| Scene | Content |
|-------|---------|
| 1 | Hook (holds 4–5 seconds) |
| 2 | The single insight (7–9 seconds) |
| 3 | CTA or open question (3–4 seconds) |

This is Bial at its most refined — three thoughts, generous holds, complete. More silence. Less filling.

---

### For Carousels

Smart Brevity's mandate: one strong idea per slide. No slide summarizes the previous one. No slide previews the next one. Each slide is a complete unit.

**Slide architecture:**

| Slide | Smart Brevity role |
|-------|-------------------|
| Cover (Slide 1) | Hook — the full argument in one line |
| Slide 2 | Lede — expand the hook by one layer |
| Slides 3–4 | Why it matters + evidence |
| Slide 5 | The implication — what this changes |
| Close slide | One line or wordmark only — no summary |

**The cover test:** Read Slide 1 only. Does it earn a swipe? If not, the problem is in the cover, not the following slides.

**Common carousel failures (and the Smart Brevity fix):**
- *Slide 2 restates the cover* → Cut Slide 2, move to evidence
- *Slide 4 summarizes Slides 2–3* → Cut it entirely — the reader was there
- *Close slide lists everything you just said* → Replace with one clean, elevating line or just the wordmark

**Carousel captions (Smart Brevity applied):**
- Line 1 = the hook from Slide 1 — verbatim or sharpened
- Line 2 = the lede (why right now, what this is)
- Line 3 = CTA
- Maximum 4 lines. No summary. No restatement.

---

### For Instagram Captions

The fitted. caption should read like a well-edited tweet from someone who knows what they're talking about — specific, complete, unhurried.

**Smart Brevity structure for captions:**

```
[Line 1 — Hook: the complete idea in one sentence]
[Line 2 — Lede or Why It Matters: one more sentence]
[CTA: two words to one sentence]
[Closer: ✦]
```

**Good example (June 15):**
> Day one looked different than I expected — in the best way. The moment the honest read lands and something clicks. That's what this is for. getfittedcareers.com ✦

**The discipline:** If Line 2 could be cut and the caption still makes its point, cut it. The shortest version that's complete is the best version.

**What Smart Brevity eliminates from captions:**
- "In this post, we're covering..."
- A sentence that says the same thing as the hook, differently
- CTAs with more than one instruction ("Follow + save + drop your thoughts below!")
- Hashtag avalanches — 3–5 maximum, always

---

### For Threads

Threads is where Smart Brevity is most naturally at home — the platform rewards densely useful, specific, personal takes that don't waste a sentence.

**Structure:**
- Sentence 1: Hook. One thought. No setup.
- Sentences 2–3: The insight — why this is true, or what this means.
- Final sentence: Open question or quiet declaration. Not a CTA.

**Maximum length:** 5 sentences. Most should be 3.

**The test:** Read it aloud. If you breathe before you're through, it's too long.

**Good example (June 7):**
> 5 things your resume signals without you realizing: title trajectory, company choices, language specificity, bullet length, and what leads each bullet. All of these are readable before the content. Worth auditing.

This is Smart Brevity: hook, evidence, implication. Done. No preamble. No summary.

---

### For Stories

Stories should be the most Bial moment in the entire fitted. ecosystem — personal, warm, minimal, and designed for the format (vertical, holds 7 seconds, disappears).

**Smart Brevity for Stories:**
- One idea per card. Never two.
- Text should be readable in 3 seconds — which means maximum 12 words on a non-poll story
- Poll options should be genuine — not tricks or obvious answers. If the poll is "Which do you prefer: A or B?" — both answers should feel plausible.
- Story sequences (5–7 cards): apply the Reel architecture — hook card, lede card, detail card, CTA card. Not 7 versions of the same idea.

**Story card types and their Smart Brevity role:**

| Type | Content | Smart Brevity role |
|------|---------|-------------------|
| Pull quote card | One line from the day's Reel or Carousel | Hook |
| Teaser card | One-line description of the day's content | Lede |
| Poll card | One genuine question, two real answers | Why It Matters (listener version) |
| Close card | Wordmark or URL only | CTA |

**What to avoid:**
- Three poll cards in a row (poll fatigue)
- A teaser card that describes the content in full — save something for the main post
- Close cards that restate the entire day's theme

## PART FIVE-B: LABEL AND GRID RULES (v2.1 additions)

### Labels are internal guides only — never post them

Content type labels (SI, SE, TS, BtS, EP, AQ, DP, AC, IM) and their associated editorial tags ("SOMETHING EDUCATIONAL," "DATA POINT / QUIET TRUTH," "INTENTIONAL MOVES," "AI CAREER COACH SPOTLIGHT," etc.) are **production references only**.

They:
- Appear in the content calendar for organizational clarity
- Appear as Reel scene direction (e.g., Scene 1 note: "[INTENTIONAL MOVES label, taupe bg]") to guide Canva layout
- Are written into cover image Canva spec notes

They **never** appear as visible text on an Instagram grid post. The cover image has one job: to stop the scroll with a single strong idea. A category badge competes with that idea and signals "content strategy," not thought.

**The exception:** The "fitted. thinks:" prompt is acceptable as a small, tracked DM Sans label (not a badge) — used sparingly on SI and AQ posts only, where it genuinely reads as a personal editorial note rather than a type indicator.

## PART SIX: FEED AESTHETIC — WARM EDITORIAL GRID

The fitted. feed should feel like the magazine shelf at a well-curated independent bookshop: diverse enough in format to stay interesting, consistent enough in aesthetic to feel like a single voice.

**Color rhythm across the feed:**
The 9 background colors should cycle in a way that prevents clusters. Never two navy posts back-to-back. Never two ink posts in the same row. The taupe posts (SE and IM) and off-white posts (BtS and AC) create breathing room — use them to air out runs of dark posts.

**Suggested 3-column grid rhythm per week:**
```
[Navy TS] [Taupe SE] [Ink SI]
[Off-white BtS] [Blue EP] [Pale Taupe AQ]
[Navy DP] [Off-white AC] [Taupe IM]
```
No two identical backgrounds in the same row. Ink and navy are adjacent only if a taupe or off-white is between them in the sequence.

**Typography rhythm:**
- DM Serif Display for most covers (authoritative, warm)
- Cormorant Garamond Italic for TS hook slides and pull-quote moments (intimate, personal)
- Mix both within a post — not both on the same cover (they compete)

**The Bial test for the grid:**
Zoom out on the feed to 9-post view. Does it feel warm and alive, like a curated visual journal — or cold and repetitive? If dark backgrounds are dominating: add more off-white and taupe posts to the queue. If it looks too similar post to post: check that the cover text is varying in size and weight across posts.
